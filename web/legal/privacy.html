3. Your Ares Empire account

One account works for every service. When you sign up I process your email address and your password (the account server stores only a hash of it, never the password itself), and, if you set one, a name. With the account I keep whether your email is confirmed, whether I approved the account, which services you may use, the provinces you put on your hub, and when the account was created and changed.

  • Why: to let you log in, to decide who may use which service, and to show you your hub.
  • Emails: you get a confirmation link, a mail when I approve your account, password-reset links, and a warning when someone logs in to your account from a new device. When you sign up, I get an email with your email address so I can approve you.
  • Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (providing the service you signed up for).
  • How long: until your account is deleted (section 12). An account I have not approved stays until I approve or delete it.

Staying logged in

After logging in you receive a login token that is valid for 30 days. On the website it is kept in a cookie (empire_session); in the apps it is kept in your iPhone's keychain, shared by the Ares Empire apps on that iPhone and readable only while it is unlocked. The cookie and the keychain entry are strictly necessary for the service you asked for (§ 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG). There are no other cookies: no tracking, no analytics.

4. Server logs and security

Whenever your browser or an app talks to my server, the server sees your device's IP address, the time and the address requested. The account server keeps a request log with IP addresses, but without your account, for 5 days, to find faults and stop abuse (for example, too many login attempts). The services behind the apps write the IP address and the address requested (which can include a search term) to the server's system log, which is kept for 14 days.

To warn you about logins from a new device, the account server keeps a fingerprint for each of the last five devices or browsers your account logged in from: a hash (MD5) of the IP address together with the browser's identification (user agent), not the IP address itself, with the time it was first and last used. When a login comes from a new fingerprint, you get an email naming that login's time, IP address and browser. The fingerprints are deleted when you change your password or your account is deleted.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. My legitimate interest is keeping the services secure and working.

5. Data on your iPhone

The apps keep some data on your iPhone so they open quickly and work offline: the login (keychain) and your account's email address for the Settings screen, a note that an app was installed (so a fresh install starts logged out), Arestoteles's copy of the notes and quick notes not yet sent, and the last figures the widgets show. Arestoteles also adds its notes to the iPhone's own search (Spotlight), on the device. All of this is deleted when you log out or remove the apps. It never leaves your iPhone except as described in this policy.

6. Push notifications (Arestemis, Aresus)

If you allow notifications, the app sends the device token that Apple assigns to it, together with your account and the app's language, to my push service. Notifications are delivered through Apple's Push Notification service (section 11), which receives the token and the text of each notification (for example the rent and area of a new flat, or which service has a problem).

  • How long: the device entry is deleted when you log out, when your account loses access, when Apple reports the token as no longer valid, or 60 days after the app last registered it. For troubleshooting I keep the last 200 notifications sent (time, account, title, text, link; no device token).
  • Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR. You decide in the iPhone's Settings whether notifications are shown, and can switch them off there at any time.

7. Arestemis (flat hunting)

Arestemis watches flat portals (WG-Gesucht, Kleinanzeigen, Immowelt) for you. It stores your search (places and areas, sizes, rents, move-in dates, portals and the "extras" you write), whether the hunt is running, your settings (your own application text, the facts in "About you", notification settings), the listings it found with their state (new, saved, applied, dismissed), the reason the AI gave, and application texts written for you. Listings are public adverts and can contain the name of a landlord or flatmate.

The AI check

Every listing that passes your exact filters is checked by an AI model against your whole search. For this, Arestemis sends your search, your "About you" text and the listing to Groq (Groq, Inc., USA). For an application written by AI it also sends your own application text and the contact name in the listing; "Describe it" sends the sentence you typed. Groq processes this data only to give the answer, under its data processing addendum (section 11). Please write in "About you" only what you would tell a landlord, and nothing sensitive (for example about health or religion). Nothing is required there.

Applications are never sent by Arestemis. You copy the text and send it yourself on the portal, where the portal's privacy policy applies. Photos of a listing load directly from the portal's server, so that portal sees your IP address; "Open listing" opens the portal's page.

  • How long: your search, the listings found for it and their states until you delete the hunt in the app; filtered-out listings 7 days; listings nobody needs any more 30 days; the list of listings already seen 180 days. Your settings until your account is deleted.
  • Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR (the search and the AI check are the service you use).

8. Contact and support

In the apps

If you write to me with the "Contact & support" form in an app, I receive the kind of message, your text, the app, its version, the iPhone's system version and type, the app's language and your account. The message is stored on the account server and sent to my mailbox by email, with your email address as the reply address. I use it only to answer and to improve the apps, and delete it 12 months after the matter is closed, or earlier with your account. The same holds when you email me directly.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR for questions about your account or the service, otherwise Art. 6(1)(f) (answering you).

The website's contact form

With the contact form you can also write to me without an account. I then receive your email address, your message, your name if you give it, and the page's language. The message is stored on the account server and sent by email (through Resend, section 11) to my mailbox at IONOS, with your email address as the reply address. I use it only to answer you, and delete it 12 months after the matter is closed. As with every request, the account server logs your IP address for 5 days (section 4); it also uses it to limit how many messages can come from one address.

Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR. My legitimate interest is answering enquiries.

Transfers outside the EU

Resend and GitHub are certified under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework, for which the EU has decided that it protects data adequately (Art. 45 GDPR). Groq is not; it is bound by the EU Standard Contractual Clauses in its customer data processing addendum, which is part of its terms (Art. 46(2)(c) GDPR). Apple transfers data from the EU to the USA under Standard Contractual Clauses as well, as its privacy policy states.
